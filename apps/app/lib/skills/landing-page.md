## Landing page

A landing page is an argument, not a stack of sections. Each block earns the next scroll.

### Structure
Use this spine, then cut anything that does not pull weight:
1. **Hero** — one specific claim (not "Build faster"), one primary CTA, one supporting proof.
2. **Proof** — logos, a metric, or a real quote. Skip it entirely rather than fake it.
3. **The problem** — name the pain in the reader's own words before pitching.
4. **How it works** — 3 steps max, concrete verbs, ideally shown not told.
5. **Depth** — the 2–3 capabilities that actually differentiate. Not a 12-item feature grid.
6. **Objection handling** — pricing, FAQ, or security, whichever blocks the sale.
7. **Close** — restate the claim, repeat the CTA.

### Hero rules
- The headline says what it IS and who it is FOR. "The AI-powered platform for modern teams"
  is a non-headline — it survives find-and-replace with any product name.
- Do not center everything by default. An asymmetric hero (text left, artifact right/bleeding
  off-canvas) reads more designed than the centered-headline-centered-subhead-centered-button
  stack.
- Ship at most two CTAs and make them visually unequal.
- Hero must be legible on a 1280×720 laptop without scrolling to find the CTA.

### Section variety
Consecutive sections must not share a layout. If section 3 is text-left/visual-right, section 4
should be full-bleed, a table, a quote, or a stepper. Alternating the same two-column layout is
the strongest template tell there is.

### Copy
- Sentence case. Specific verbs. No "Empower", "Seamless", "Unlock", "Revolutionize", "Elevate".
- Subheads carry information, not filler — if you can delete it with no loss, delete it.
- Feature names should be nouns a user would say out loud.

### Craft
- Give the page one signature move: a distinctive type treatment, a recurring motif, an unusual
  but readable grid. One. Not five.
- Footer is real: navigation, legal, contact. Not three dead placeholder columns.
- Responsive is a design pass, not a `flex-col` fallback — reconsider hierarchy at mobile width.
